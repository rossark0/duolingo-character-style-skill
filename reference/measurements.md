# Measurements from the guide's own drawings

Source: Duolingo brand guide, illustration pages: "Shape language", "Characters" and "Posing". The old address design.duolingo.com/illustration now redirects to the blog. An archived copy is at web.archive.org/web/20260514160843/https://design.duolingo.com/illustration/shape-language.

The site is a JavaScript app, so the text and the SVG drawings live in its bundle. Every number below was read from those SVG files: units are the drawing's own, and "x100" scales them to a 100-wide head.

## Quotes

- "All of our illustrations are made from three basic shapes: the rounded rectangle, the circle, and the rounded triangle. You'll probably find yourself using the rounded rectangle the most. [...] Just make sure that every shape has rounded edges. Pointy shapes are off-brand."
- "It's the rhythm of simple shapes that makes our illustrations interesting. [...] Shapes with similar visual weight are predictable and uninteresting."
- "Usually, the head and body are composed of 1-2 basic shapes each."
- Shoulders: "Ensure that the arm socket is always concealed behind the torso." and "Avoid too many shapes. It complicates the overall silhouette."
- Arms and hands: "These can be the most time-consuming parts for illustrators and animators when posing and animating. Try to keep hands as abstract as you can by keeping them as circles in most places. If you need to show fingers, display the bare minimum needed for the pose."
- Posing: "As much as possible, try not to use characters in a static, expressionless state, which could make them feel lifeless."
- Hair: "Hair should be simple but interesting. [...] If possible, compose hair with just one or two large shapes." and "Ensure that you include some kind of hair above the ear/sideburn in your hairstyle."
- Facial hair: "When adding facial hair, keep the nose the same color as the skin."
- Color: "Keep your illustration to a few colors. Too many colors can hurt legibility when an object scales to a small size."
- Floating accents: "Only float an object if it serves the illustration."
- Duo's joints: "Duo can bend at the waist, elbow, and wrists. When bending at the waist, use a rounded rectangle to place over his body instead of editing his existing shape."

Scaled to a 100-wide head:
- arm 36 to 39;
- neck 33 to 35;
- torso 138 to 155;
- hand diameter 48 to 54;
- hanging arm 144;
- bent segments 55 to 80;
- raised straight arm up to 220.

Shoulder placement (center of the arm's round end):
- 13 to 19 units in from the torso's side edge (0.6 to 0.9 x the arm thickness);
- 20 to 29 units below the torso's top (0.25 to 0.35 x the torso width).

Drawing order in the guide's files:
- the hands are drawn before the arms in the hanging figure, and after them in the raise-hand figure (same color, so either works);
- the torso is drawn after the arms behind it.

## Real characters ("Posing", "Characters")

**A turbaned man eating noodles:**
- sweater #D93868, its one shade #C5305C;
- sleeve 23.6 thick;
- hand diameter 23 (0.97 x the sleeve);
- face 41.6 wide;
- the sleeves in front of the sweater are the same color and merge with it. The elbows stick out of the silhouette.

**The same man sneezing:**
- straight hanging sleeves, 23.6 thick;
- hand circles 23 wide;
- each hand is drawn before its sleeve, so the sleeve end covers about 3 units of the hand.

**A woman in a yellow sweater with crossed arms:**
- same-color sleeves merged with the torso;
- the two hands show at the sides of the chest;
- the elbows make small bumps.

**A woman waving:**
- bare arm;
- four spread finger capsules;
- the other arm hangs behind the torso.
